# Food showcase: photo research and moving gallery

Marvin asked on 2026-10-06 for food-photo research and a moving showcase inspired by the supplied Horai restaurant video, with discretion to adapt it to Table Reserve. The guest menu already works from MySQL; the aim is to make that experience more inviting without changing reservation or ordering rules.

## Recommendation and selection method

Use six credited local photographs in an editorial gallery between the booking hero and room cards. Keep the ivory type, warm gold, charcoal background and generous spacing. The actual menu is Filipino/coastal and hearth-led, so sushi-heavy imagery from the reference would not represent this catalog well.

Searched Pexels and Unsplash, inspected photo pages and the Pexels license, downloaded ten candidates, and compared their rendered crops in a contact sheet. This is a curated selection from the researched candidates, not an exhaustive claim about every available food photograph. Selection is design judgment using four criteria: connection to the real menu's ingredients/categories, compatibility with the warm palette, readable subjects in narrow portrait cards, and a verifiable free website-use license. Raw stock images need not be artificially darkened; readable captions use a local CSS gradient.

This implementation uses food inspiration in a fictional venue website and credits each photographer. TikTok frames, competitor artwork, logos and paid-library photographs are not used as product assets.

## Motion adapted to this application

The full reference review is in `research/08_ui_motion_research.md` section 15. It establishes a multicolumn photo gallery and editorial food presentation; exact animation timings and sticky/scroll mechanics were not verified. The following behavior is our adaptation:

- Desktop: three columns move gently using CSS transforms, with the middle column travelling in the opposite direction. Each loop lasts 40–46 seconds. Decorative repeat copies are hidden from assistive technology and contain no interactive controls.
- Pause/Resume works with mouse, touch and keyboard. Hovering or focusing the gallery temporarily stops movement. Explicit pause survives viewport and motion-preference changes within the page.
- An Intersection Observer starts movement only while the gallery intersects the viewport. The document visibility event pauses hidden-page motion. Missing observer support retains the complete static layout.
- At 760px and below: a native horizontal strip supports touch scrolling and keyboard arrows, with a visible swipe hint and no autoplay.
- Reduced motion: a complete static gallery, with no automatic movement or unnecessary motion button. JavaScript failure/disablement also leaves the photographs, captions, credits and menu link available.
- Failed images leave stable card geometry and readable captions. Explore the menu opens the existing database menu. Browsing photography creates no hold, reservation or order.

The pause mechanism follows [W3C Pause, Stop, Hide guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide). Preference handling follows [MDN prefers-reduced-motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion). Visibility scheduling follows the [Intersection Observer API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Intersection_Observer_API). These explain the selected implementation; automated browser checks do not establish full accessibility certification or cross-browser performance.
